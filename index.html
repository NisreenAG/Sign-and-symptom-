Signs and symptoms

An interactive lesson: five activities, then a group role play.

1. Starter: spot the difference

Work alone for one minute, then compare with a partner.

Case: Maria tells the nurse, “I feel dizzy and I’m so tired.” The nurse then wraps a cuff on her arm: blood pressure 85/50. Her skin looks pale and cool.

Question: Which pieces of information came from Maria, and which did the nurse find for herself? Write your two lists, then reveal the answer.

Symptoms (Maria reports)

Dizziness, tiredness. Only Maria can feel them.

Signs (nurse finds)

Blood pressure 85/50, pale skin, cool skin. Anyone can observe or measure them.

2. Key ideas

Symptom

What the patient feels and reports. It is subjective.
Examples: pain, nausea, fatigue, itching, dizziness.

Sign

What someone else can observe or measure. It is objective.
Examples: fever reading, rash, swelling, pulse rate, wheezing heard with a stethoscope.

Remember:

  • One condition often has both. Asthma: shortness of breath (symptom) and wheezing (sign).
  • Some things are both. “I feel hot” is a symptom; a thermometer reading of 39.2 °C is a sign.
  • A condition can have signs with no symptoms, such as high blood pressure in the early stage.
  • Good care records both, clearly separated, in the patient’s own words for symptoms.
